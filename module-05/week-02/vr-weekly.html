Gas-Stoichiometry & Partial-Pressure Station
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~40 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Gas-Stoichiometry & Partial-Pressure Station Diagram (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're continuing the project you build across all four weeks of this module: the Gas Behavior & Energy Lab, which you'll narrate on camera at the module's end. Last week (Build 1) you built the gas-law apparatus — a piston/cylinder with a movable plunger and gauges for pressure, volume, and temperature — and demonstrated Boyle's law. This week you add two things beside it: a gas-stoichiometry station showing whole-number volumes reacting in ratio, and a partial-pressure jar showing two gases mixed so their pressures add. This is Build 2 of 4. The big idea to show: for gases, equal volumes mean equal counts — so a volume ratio is a mole ratio — and when gases share a container, their pressures simply add.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your Gas_Wk1_YourName snapshot from last week, plus your notes from Lessons 5.03–5.04 (gas stoichiometry and partial pressures).
▶  About 40 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Gas & Energy Lab
Spawn point: Lab Entrance
When you arrive: your Week-1 gas-law apparatus (piston/cylinder with P, V, T gauges and your labeled Boyle's-law pair) is still standing, exactly where you left it. A Whiteboard IFX shows this week's Step 1 and an empty reaction bench with labeled volume trays and a clear two-chamber jar prop. A sticky note at your feet reads “Start here.” You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
You'll move through an investigator's arc — Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend — then finish with a short written reflection. Build first, but reason as you go.
1
Observe. Read the Whiteboard IFX and find the reaction bench beside your Week-1 apparatus. Confirm your gas-law apparatus is still standing — you'll reference it on camera later. Notice the empty volume trays and the clear two-chamber jar prop waiting on the bench.
2
Question. Hold the guiding question the Whiteboard poses: when gases react or mix, does the space they take up tell you how many molecules are involved? Keep it in mind as you build — everything on this bench is a way of answering it.
3
Predict. Before you build, place a sticky note with two predictions and sign it: (a) for 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O, what will the volume ratio of the three gases look like? (b) When two gases share the jar, will the total pressure be the sum, the average, or the larger of the two? Commit before you find out.
4
Investigate. Build the gas-stoichiometry station: line up three volume blocks for 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O — make the H2 and H2O stacks visibly twice the O2 stack, so the 2 : 1 : 2 volume ratio is something you can see. Then spawn the partial-pressure jar, fill it with two gases (for example, N2 and O2), and use the 3D pen to label each gas's partial pressure — e.g., “P(N2) = 0.60 atm” and “P(O2) = 0.40 atm.”
5
Analyze. With the 3D pen or a sticky note, label the station “equal volumes → equal moles, so volume ratio = mole ratio” and write the matched amounts (“2 vol H2 + 1 vol O2 → 2 vol H2O”). Beside the jar, show Dalton's law adding up: Ptotal = P(N2) + P(O2) = 0.60 + 0.40 = 1.00 atm. Now return to your prediction note and mark where you were right and where you were surprised.
6
Defend. Add one signed reasoning sticky note that uses your build as evidence: why does a volume ratio equal a mole ratio, and why do two gases' pressures add instead of averaging? Point to the blocks and the jar as you write — you are making an argument from what you built, not just stating a rule.
7
Extend. On a final sticky note, connect the bench to the wider world: name one real place gases are metered by volume (for example, ammonia synthesis or medical gas mixtures), and write one question you'd want to test next. Leave room to the right for next week's KMT-particle build (Weeks 3–4).
8
Save & capture. Lock the new station and jar so they aren't bumped, take a snapshot of the whole bench and save it as Gas_Wk2_YourName (your saved progress for next week), and take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows the labeled volume ratio, the partial-pressure jar, and your prediction and reasoning notes.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked objects will be waiting when you return. Save the Gas_Wk2_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
