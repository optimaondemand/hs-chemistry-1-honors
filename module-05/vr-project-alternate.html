📚 THE BIG PICTURE
Across this module you've assembled a Gas Behavior & Energy Lab — on slides or as physical models — one week at a time: Week 1 the gas-law apparatus (a piston/cylinder with P, V, T gauges, demonstrating Boyle's law); Week 2 a gas-stoichiometry and partial-pressure station; Week 3 kinetic-molecular-theory particle annotations and an effusion observation; Week 4 a calorimetry setup (a hot metal in water with a thermometer).
Now you record a narrated walkthrough of your build. The graded work is your spoken explanation of the chemistry, not the artwork — the model is the stage; your reasoning is the performance.
⭐ WHY THIS MATTERS
This walkthrough is one of only two teacher-reviewed pieces in Module 5 (the other is your calorimetry lab report). Your teacher will watch your recording and respond to your reasoning personally.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (screen-recorded slides)
Use your four weekly PowerPoint builds as the visual. Record your screen while you narrate a tour through the slides: the gas-law apparatus + Boyle, the gas-stoichiometry + partial-pressure station, the KMT annotations + effusion, and the calorimetry setup + conservation of energy.
Option B — Hands-on (physical models)
Use a syringe or sealed-plunger model for the gas laws, drawings or beads for particles and partial pressures, and a real cup of hot-then-cooling water with a thermometer for calorimetry. Record a video narrating the same tour.
🎤 WHAT YOUR RECORDING MUST COVER (aim for 3–5 minutes)
1
Open with the big question: can chemical behavior be measured — can we put numbers on how a gas pushes back and on how energy moves?
2
The gas laws & PV = nRT (Week 1). Show your apparatus and demonstrate Boyle's law (P up, V down at constant T — e.g., 1.0 atm × 2.0 L → 4.0 atm × 0.50 L). Explain how the variables tie together in PV = nRT (temperature in kelvin).
3
Gas stoichiometry & Dalton's partial pressures (Week 2). Show volumes reacting in whole-number ratios (2 vol H2 + 1 vol O2 → 2 vol H2O, by Avogadro's principle), and explain Dalton's law — mixed gases give a total pressure equal to the sum of their partials (e.g., 0.60 atm + 0.40 atm = 1.00 atm).
4
KMT explains the laws + Graham's law (Week 3). Explain that the kinetic-molecular theory — particles in constant motion, average kinetic energy set by temperature (kelvin) — is why the measured laws hold, and use Graham's law to say why lighter molecules effuse faster (He vs O2: √(32.00/4.00) = √8 ≈ 2.83× faster).
5
Energy, calorimetry & conservation of energy (Week 4). Explain q = mcΔT with your calorimeter — a hot metal dropped into cooler water until they share one temperature — and that by the first law the heat lost by the metal equals the heat gained by the water (qmetal = −qwater): the energy books must balance.
6
Close by explaining the evidence — then name the limits. Tie each claim back to what your model actually shows: the P/V/T readings are your evidence for the gas laws and PV = nRT; the particles are why the KMT explains those laws (a measured law vs the theory behind it); the thermometer is your evidence that energy is conserved (qmetal = −qwater). Then state one honest limit — the ideal-gas model breaks down at high pressure and low temperature, and a real calorimeter's energy books don't perfectly close because heat leaks to the cup and air. End on the through-line: measurement plus conservation is the discipline of the quantitative.
