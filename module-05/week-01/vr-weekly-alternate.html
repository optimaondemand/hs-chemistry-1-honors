📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You'll build a Gas Behavior & Energy Lab — a set of gas and energy diagrams, built up across four weeks, that you'll explain at the end of the module — using a PowerPoint deck or a hand-drawn poster. This week you draw the centerpiece: a gas-law apparatus, a piston/cylinder with a movable plunger and three gauges for pressure (P), volume (V), and temperature (T). You'll then show Boyle's law with a two-panel “before / after”: a large volume at low pressure, then a small volume at high pressure, with one matching P–V pair labeled. In later weeks you'll add a measured-amounts / partial-pressure station, particle annotations, and a calorimetry setup, then record a short narrated walkthrough.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Make a 3-slide deck: a title slide, a slide showing the labeled apparatus (piston/cylinder + P, V, T gauges), and a slide with the Boyle “before / after” two-panel and the labeled P–V pair.
Option B — Hands-on (poster)
Draw the apparatus on paper with its three labeled gauges, add a “before / after” Boyle sketch and the P–V pair underneath, and photograph the poster into one document.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
These steps follow the way a scientist actually investigates: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Don't skip the predict step — committing to a prediction before you draw the “after” panel is the point.
1
Observe. Title your project Gas Behavior & Energy Lab, note the master equation PV = nRT, and draw the gas-law apparatus: a cylinder with a movable plunger and three gauges labeled P (atm), V (L), and T (K). Show it at rest — volume 2.0 L, pressure about 1.0 atm. Leave room for the Week 2–4 exhibits.
2
Question. Write the question you are investigating on the slide or poster: “How does the pressure change as the volume changes?”
3
Predict — before you draw the “after” panel. Write your prediction: when the volume is squeezed to 0.50 L (one-quarter), what will the pressure be, and why? Commit to a number, and don't change it after you work out the answer.
4
Investigate. Draw Boyle's law as “before / after”: panel 1 = plunger out, large volume, low pressure (2.0 L at 1.0 atm); panel 2 = plunger pushed in, small volume, high pressure (0.50 L at 4.0 atm).
5
Analyze. Check the product: 1.0 atm × 2.0 L = 2.0, and 4.0 atm × 0.50 L = 2.0 — the product P × V stayed the same. Compare the result with the prediction you committed in step 3.
6
Defend. Add a signed claim caption with evidence and reasoning: “Claim: P and V are inversely proportional. Evidence: 1.0 atm at 2.0 L → 4.0 atm at 0.50 L. Reasoning: the product P×V is constant (Boyle: P1V1 = P2V2).” Sign your name.
7
Extend. Add a short note predicting what would happen to the pressure if you instead heated the gas in a sealed, rigid container (volume fixed) — and name the variable doing the work this time.
💾
Save. Save your finished file (deck or photo document). Keep it — you'll add the measured-amounts station, particle annotations, and a calorimetry setup in Weeks 2–4.
