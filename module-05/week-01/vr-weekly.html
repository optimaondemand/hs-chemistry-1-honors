Week 1 · OptimaXR
Build the Gas-Law Apparatus — Begin Your Gas Behavior & Energy Lab
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Build the Gas-Law Apparatus (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're starting the project you'll build across all four weeks of this module: a Gas Behavior & Energy Lab — a working bench of gas and energy instruments that you'll narrate on camera at the end of the module. This week you build the centerpiece: a gas-law apparatus, a piston/cylinder with a movable plunger and three gauges — pressure (P), volume (V), and temperature (T). Once it's built, you'll demonstrate Boyle's law: push the plunger in to shrink the volume, watch the pressure gauge climb, and label one matching pressure–volume pair on the apparatus. In later weeks you'll add a measured-amounts / partial-pressure station, particle annotations for kinetic-molecular theory, and a calorimetry setup — then record the full walkthrough.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lessons 5.01–5.02 (pressure units, and Boyle's law P1V1 = P2V2).
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Gas & Energy Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: a Whiteboard IFX facing the bench shows PV = nRT and this week's Step 1, beside an empty mounting plate labeled Gas-Law Apparatus and three blank gauge faces marked P, V, and T. A sticky note at your feet reads “Start here.” You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
These steps follow the way a scientist actually investigates: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Don't skip the predict step — committing to a prediction before you push the plunger is the point.
1
Observe. Read the Whiteboard IFX. From the IFX object library, spawn the piston/cylinder onto the mounting plate (a clear cylinder with a movable plunger) and attach the three gauges, labeling them P (atm), V (L), and T (K) — the variables of PV = nRT made physical. Set the plunger so the volume reads 2.0 L and note the resting pressure (about 1.0 atm). Leave open bench space to the right for the Week 2–4 stations.
2
Question. On a sticky note, write the question you are about to investigate: “How does the pressure gauge respond as I change the gas's volume?” Place it on the bench beside the apparatus.
3
Predict — before you touch the plunger. On a second sticky note, commit a prediction: when you push the volume down to 0.50 L (one-quarter of the way), what will the pressure gauge read, and why? Write an actual number. This note is your committed prediction — do not change it after you see the result.
4
Investigate. Now push the plunger in until the volume reads 0.50 L and watch the pressure gauge climb (to about 4.0 atm). Read and remember both pressure–volume pairs.
5
Analyze. Compare your two pairs: 1.0 atm × 2.0 L = 2.0, and 4.0 atm × 0.50 L = 2.0 — the product P × V stayed the same. Check the result against your committed prediction from step 3. Was it right? Off? By how much?
6
Defend. Place a signed sticky note that states a claim with its evidence and reasoning: “Claim: P and V are inversely proportional. Evidence: 1.0 atm at 2.0 L → 4.0 atm at 0.50 L. Reasoning: the product P×V is constant (Boyle: P1V1 = P2V2).” Sign your name.
7
Extend. On one last sticky note, predict what would happen to the pressure if you instead heated this gas in a sealed, rigid cylinder (volume fixed) — and name the variable that would be doing the work this time. You'll build toward that relationship as the lab grows.
💾
Save & capture. Lock the apparatus and gauges so they aren't bumped, save a snapshot named Gas_Wk1_YourName (your project's saved progress for next week), and take one in-headset screenshot showing the apparatus, its three labeled P/V/T gauges, and your claim note. That screenshot is what you'll submit.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked apparatus will be waiting when you return. Save the Gas_Wk1_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot of the gas-law apparatus, its three labeled P/V/T gauges, and your claim note to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write a short CER reflection (2–4 sentences): a Claim about how pressure and volume relate, the Evidence (the pressure–volume pair you measured), and your Reasoning (why the product P×V staying constant supports the claim). Add one sentence comparing the result to the prediction you committed in step 3. (This reflection is used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
