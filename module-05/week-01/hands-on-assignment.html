📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
From this week's lessons: measuring and converting pressure (5.01), and the gas laws — Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, the combined gas law, and the ideal gas law PV = nRT (5.02).
This is your week's “doing chemistry” work: eight problems across every gas relationship you just learned, bracketed by a short reason-in-words step before you calculate and a data-analysis write-up after.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Given some of a gas's conditions, can I predict the rest — and prove the answer is right by watching my units (and my Kelvin temperatures) behave?
🧮 YOUR TOOLKIT (USE THESE EXACT VALUES)
Pressure equivalences: 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr = 101.3 kPa = 14.7 psi
Ideal gas constant: R = 0.08206 L·atm·mol−1·K−1
Temperature: K = °C + 273  (gas laws require Kelvin)
STP: 273 K and 1 atm · molar volume = 22.4 L/mol
Working forms: Boyle P1V1=P2V2 · Charles V1/T1=V2/T2 · Gay-Lussac P1/T1=P2/T2 · Combined P1V1/T1=P2V2/T2 · Ideal PV=nRT

✎️ BEFORE YOU CALCULATE · REASON IN WORDS FIRST
Chemists commit to a prediction before they compute. For problem 3 (Boyle) and problem 5 (Gay-Lussac), first write one sentence each, in words with no arithmetic: name the relationship (direct or inverse), say which variable you are solving for, and predict which way it should move and roughly how much. Put these two sentences at the top of your submission, then work the problems and check whether your numbers agree with your words.
▶  Check your reasoning — sample sentences
Problem 3 (Boyle): “P and V are inversely related at constant T, and I'm solving for the new pressure; because the volume is cut to one-quarter (2.0 L → 0.50 L), the pressure should rise about four-fold.”  Problem 5 (Gay-Lussac): “P and Kelvin T are directly related at constant V, and I'm solving for the new pressure; because the temperature rises from 300 K to 450 K (a factor of 1.5), the pressure should rise by about half.” If a computed answer contradicts a sound prediction like these, re-check the setup before you trust the number.
🧮 PART A · PRESSURE CONVERSIONS (problems 1–2)
1. A weather report gives the pressure as 0.50 atm. Convert this to mmHg and to kPa.
2. A tank gauge reads 1520 mmHg. What is this pressure in atm?
▶  Check your thinking — Part A answer key
1. 0.50 atm × (760 mmHg / 1 atm) = 380 mmHg;   0.50 atm × (101.3 kPa / 1 atm) = 50.7 kPa.
2. 1520 mmHg × (1 atm / 760 mmHg) = 2.0 atm (mmHg cancels, leaving atm).
💉 PART B · BOYLE & CHARLES (problems 3–4)
3. (Boyle) A gas at 1.0 atm occupies 2.0 L. At constant temperature it is compressed to 0.50 L. Find the new pressure.
4. (Charles) A gas occupies 2.0 L at 300 K. At constant pressure it is heated to 600 K. Find the new volume.
▶  Check your thinking — Part B answer key
3. P2 = P1V1/V2 = (1.0 atm × 2.0 L)/0.50 L = 4.0 atm (volume to one-quarter → pressure × 4).
4. V2 = V1T2/T1 = (2.0 L × 600 K)/300 K = 4.0 L (double the Kelvin temperature → double the volume).
🔥 PART C · GAY-LUSSAC & THE COMBINED LAW (problems 5–6)
5. (Gay-Lussac) A rigid tank holds gas at 1.0 atm and 300 K. It is heated to 450 K. Find the new pressure.
6. (Combined) A gas occupies 2.0 L at 1.0 atm and 300 K. The conditions change to 1.0 L and 400 K. Find the new pressure.
▶  Check your thinking — Part C answer key
5. P2 = P1T2/T1 = (1.0 atm × 450 K)/300 K = 1.5 atm.
6. P2 = (P1V1T2)/(T1V2) = (1.0 atm × 2.0 L × 400 K)/(300 K × 1.0 L) = 800/300 = 2.67 atm (about 2.7 atm). Both halving the volume and warming the gas push the pressure up.
🔭 PART D · THE IDEAL GAS LAW, PV = nRT (problems 7–8)
7. What volume does 1.00 mol of an ideal gas occupy at STP (273 K, 1.00 atm)? Solve PV = nRT for V using R = 0.08206.
8. How many moles of gas are present in 2.0 L at 1.0 atm and 273 K? Solve PV = nRT for n.
▶  Check your thinking — Part D worked answers
7. V = nRT/P = (1.00 mol × 0.08206 × 273 K)/1.00 atm = 22.4 L — the molar volume of an ideal gas at STP.
8. n = PV/RT = (1.0 atm × 2.0 L)/(0.08206 × 273 K) = 2.0/22.4 = 0.0893 mol. (Sense check: 2.0 L is just under one-tenth of 22.4 L, so a bit under 0.1 mol is right.)

Check yourself: did every temperature go in as Kelvin, and did your units cancel to leave exactly the unit you were asked for?
📝 PART E · ANALYZE YOUR RESULT IN WORDS (problem 9)
9. Look back at your answer to problem 6 (the combined gas law): the pressure rose from 1.0 atm to about 2.7 atm. In 2–3 sentences, explain why it climbed by separating the two causes — what the volume change did to the pressure, and what the temperature change did — and say which effect was larger. Use your actual numbers as the evidence.
▶  Check your thinking — sample analysis
Two changes pushed the pressure up at once. First, the volume was halved (2.0 L → 1.0 L); by Boyle's inverse relationship, halving the volume alone would about double the pressure (to ≈ 2.0 atm). Second, the Kelvin temperature rose from 300 K to 400 K (a factor of 4/3), which by Gay-Lussac's direct relationship multiplies the pressure by about 1.33. Together: 1.0 atm × 2 × (400/300) ≈ 2.7 atm. The volume change was the larger of the two effects (×2 versus ×1.33).
