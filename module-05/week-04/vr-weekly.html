You're completing the Gas & Energy Lab you've built across this module. 🔬 START AS A SCIENTIST · OBSERVE → QUESTION → PREDICT
This build follows the same investigative arc you use throughout the course — Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Do the first three before you spawn anything, and jot them on a sticky note in the Session:
Observe. A hot metal is lowered into cooler water; over a minute or two the water warms and the metal cools until both settle at one shared temperature.
Question. How much energy actually moved from the metal to the water — and does every joule the metal loses show up in the water?
Predict. Commit before building: will |qmetal| come out exactly equal to qwater, a little less, or a little more? Say why in one sentence — you'll test this against your own numbers below.
📝 INVESTIGATE → ANALYZE → DEFEND → EXTEND (WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER)
Steps 1–3 are your Investigate (build and set up the measurement); step 4's worked number is your Analyze; the rehearsal in step 6 is where you Defend the conservation claim with evidence; and connecting the calorimetry station back to the whole four-station lab is your Extend.
1
In the Calorimetry Station staging area, spawn a coffee-cup calorimeter (an insulated cup of water with a lid) and a thermometer. Group them so the setup is obvious on camera.
2
Spawn a hot metal block and lower it into the water. Use the 3D pen to draw an arrow showing heat flowing from the metal into the water, and label the cup with the water's start and final temperatures.
3
Add a sticky note with the equation q = m·c·ΔT, and a second sticky note stating the conservation rule: heat lost by the metal = heat gained by the water (qmetal = −qwater).
4
Add a worked-example sticky note: 50.0 g water, 20.0 → 23.5 °C → q = (100.0)(4.184)(3.5) = 1,464 J gained by the water = 1,464 J lost by the metal. (Use your own calorimetry numbers from Lesson 5.07 or the hands-on if you prefer.)
5
Lock the full exhibit (all four stations — gas-law apparatus, stoichiometry/partial-pressure, KMT, and now calorimetry), then take a snapshot and save it as Gas_Final_YourName. This is your project's completed state.
6
Rehearse your walkthrough out loud once, moving station to station: the gas-law apparatus (P, V, T), measured amounts & partial pressures, the KMT particle picture, and finally the calorimetry station and conservation of energy. This is the narration you'll record for the graded module project (point to the module VR project assignment next).
7
Take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows the finished calorimetry station (calorimeter, hot metal, thermometer, and the q = m·c·ΔT / conservation notes). That screenshot is what you'll submit for this weekly assignment.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your locked exhibit will be waiting when you return. Save the Gas_Final_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost — you'll want it for the recording.
✎ CER REFLECTION (REQUIRED · 3–4 SENTENCES)
Before you submit, write a short Claim – Evidence – Reasoning reflection (on a Session sticky note and in the Canvas comment box):

Claim. State what your calorimetry station demonstrates about energy (for example, that energy is conserved as heat moves from the metal to the water).
Evidence. Cite your worked number — the heat gained by the water and the heat lost by the metal.
Reasoning. Explain why the two are equal and opposite: connect it to the first law and q = m·c·ΔT. Then revisit your Predict step — did |qmetal| equal qwater, and what would a small gap in a real, imperfectly insulated cup tell you?
