Week 3 · OptimaXR
See the Particles Move
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “See the Particles Move (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're on the third week of your module-long project: the Gas & Energy Lab in OptimaXR, which you'll narrate on camera at module's end. In Week 1 you built the gas-law apparatus (piston/cylinder with P, V, and T gauges); in Week 2 you added a measured-amounts station with a partial-pressure jar. This week you make the kinetic-molecular theory visible: spawn a swarm of moving particles inside the apparatus, annotate it with two sticky notes capturing the heart of Lesson 5.05, and set up a small diffusion/effusion observation that shows lighter particles outrunning heavier ones. Today's question: what are the particles actually doing that makes every gauge in your apparatus read the way it does?
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lessons 5.05 and 5.06 (KMT postulates; Graham's law; where real gases deviate).
▶  Your saved Gas_Wk2_YourName progress from last week.
▶  About 35 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Gas & Energy Lab
Spawn point: Gas-Law Apparatus
When you arrive: the gas-law apparatus and measured-amounts station you built in Weeks 1–2 are waiting where you left them. A Whiteboard IFX by the apparatus shows this week's task — bring the gas to life by filling the cylinder with moving particles and explaining what they are doing. A sticky note reads “Start here.”
📝 YOUR INVESTIGATION, IN ORDER
Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend — the same arc a scientist uses at the bench.
OBSERVE
1
At the Gas-Law Apparatus, look at the P, V, and T gauges you built in Weeks 1–2 and read the Whiteboard IFX. Notice that the gauges give you numbers but no reason.
QUESTION
2
Place a sticky note above the apparatus with today's driving question: “What are the particles doing that makes these gauges read the way they do — and why would a lighter gas escape faster?”
PREDICT
3
Before you build anything, add a second sticky note with your prediction: if you release a light gas and a heavy gas at the same time, which one spreads and escapes faster? Commit to an answer now — you'll check it against what you build.
INVESTIGATE
4
Spawn 10–15 small particle spheres inside the cylinder and spread them far apart, to show that a gas is mostly empty space. Use the IFX motion/animation tool (or hand-place and nudge them) so they appear to be in constant, random motion.
5
Set up a small diffusion/effusion observation beside the cylinder: spawn two clusters — a few light particles (label “He”) and a few heavy ones (label “O₂” or “Cl₂”) — with a small pinhole or gap they can spread through. Make the light cluster spread/escape faster than the heavy one.
ANALYZE
6
Compare what you built to your prediction. Then capture the explanation on two sticky notes: one reading “avg KE ∝ T (Kelvin)” with a 3D-pen arrow to the swarm, and one on the diffusion/effusion setup reading “lighter molecule = faster, Graham's law” with rate₁/rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁). Sign the second note.
DEFEND
7
Lock the swarm and notes so they aren't bumped, save a snapshot named Gas_Wk3_YourName, and take one screenshot that clearly shows the particle swarm, both sticky notes, and the diffusion/effusion setup — the evidence for your explanation. (You'll defend it in words in the CER reflection below.)
EXTEND
8
Add one last optional sticky note connecting the kinetic picture beyond the apparatus — e.g., “same particle motion drives atmospheric mixing” or “real gases have size + attractions → PV = nRT strains at high P, low T.” Then leave open floor space beside the apparatus for next week's calorimetry setup and the final recording.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked particles and notes will be waiting when you return. Save the Gas_Wk3_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost — next week you'll add the calorimetry setup and record the full narrated walkthrough.
📝 REQUIRED · CER REFLECTION
In the Canvas comment box (or a short typed note), defend what you built with a three-part Claim–Evidence–Reasoning reflection:
▶  Claim: Which of your two labeled gases moves faster at the same temperature?
▶  Evidence: What did you observe in your diffusion/effusion setup, and what does Graham's law give for that pair's ratio?
▶  Reasoning: Why does the kinetic-molecular theory predict this? (Tie it to equal average kinetic energy at one temperature and to ½mv².) Did the result match your PREDICT-step guess?
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot of the particle swarm, both sticky notes, and the diffusion/effusion setup to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write your CER reflection (the Claim–Evidence–Reasoning block above): which labeled gas moves faster, your evidence, and the kinetic-molecular reasoning behind it. (Your reflection may be used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
