Week 3 · OptimaXR
Add Property-Explanation Annotations — Finish Your IMF & States Model
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Add Property-Explanation Annotations (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
This is the final build of your module-long Intermolecular Forces & States Model. Over the last two weeks you built molecular models of the three intermolecular forces (Week 1) and a crystal lattice with a dissolution station (Week 2). This week you connect the micro to the macro: you'll add property-explanation annotations — short labeled notes that tie each model to a real, observable property it causes. After this build, your model is complete and ready for the recorded walkthrough that is your module project. No new objects this week — the work is the explaining.
🔬 WORK LIKE AN INVESTIGATOR
This build is a short investigation, not just a labeling job. Move through the arc as you work:
● Observe your three finished models.  ● Question: which observable property does each one cause?  ● Predict the property in your head before you write the note.  ● Investigate by placing each annotation and arrow (the steps below).  ● Analyze whether your “cause → effect” link truly holds.  ● Defend one link in the CER reflection you submit.  ● Extend: name one more property your model could explain.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your existing model from Weeks 1–2 (IMF models + lattice + dissolution station) in the persistent Session.
▶  Your notes from Lessons 6.01–6.07 (IMFs → properties; lattice types → properties; colligative freezing-point depression ΔTf = i · Kf · m).
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects from Weeks 1–2 are still there)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: States & Solutions Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: a Whiteboard IFX facing the bench shows this week's task — “Connect each model to a property it explains” — beside three empty sticky-note anchors pointing at (1) your water/hydrogen-bond model, (2) your ionic lattice, and (3) your dissolution station. Your Week 1–2 builds are exactly where you left them. You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Walk your bench and confirm your earlier builds are intact: the three IMF models, the crystal lattice, and the dissolution station. Re-lock anything that looks bumped.
2
On your hydrogen-bond / water model, add a sticky note connecting it to a real property: e.g. “Hydrogen bonds between water molecules → high boiling point, and ice floats (open H-bonded lattice is less dense than liquid).”
3
On your ionic lattice, add a note linking structure to property: e.g. “Strong ionic bonds in a rigid lattice → high melting point; brittle (shifting a layer brings like charges together and it shatters).”
4
At your dissolution station, add a colligative note: e.g. “Dissolved salt lowers water's freezing point: ΔTf = i · Kf · m. For 1.00 m NaCl, ΔTf = 2 × 1.86 × 1.00 = 3.72 °C, so it freezes at −3.72 °C — why salt melts ice on roads.”
5
Use the 3D pen to draw a quick arrow from each model to its property note, so the “cause → effect” link is visible. Sign your name on one of the notes.
6
Lock all your annotations, then take a snapshot and save it as IMF_Wk3_YourName. Because this is the final build, save a second snapshot of the whole completed model as IMF_Final_YourName — that's the one you'll narrate for the module project.
7
Take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows at least two of your model→property annotations (with the arrows). That screenshot is what you'll submit.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your locked annotations will be waiting when you return. Save the IMF_Wk3_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot showing at least two model→property annotations (with arrows) to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write a one-sentence caption: name one model and the macroscopic property it explains. (Your caption will be used when your work appears in the class gallery walk, and your IMF_Final_YourName model is what you'll record for the module project.)
3. CER reflection (3–4 sentences). Pick one of your model→property links and defend it: Claim — state the property; Evidence — the structural feature in your model (the hydrogen bonds, the ionic lattice, or the dissolved ions with ΔTf = i · Kf · m); Reasoning — why that structure produces that property. Type it in the comment box or a short text post.
