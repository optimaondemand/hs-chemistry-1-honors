📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You'll continue your Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station — a set of diagrams built up across the module — using a PowerPoint deck or a hand-drawn poster. This is Build 3 of 5. You'll work like a scientist: observe a balanced reaction, commit a prediction for how it will react before you diagram, then build a stress panel showing how the equilibrium shifts to partially counteract three stresses — an added reactant, a pressure change, and a temperature change — each with the shift direction labeled, plus a note that a catalyst does not shift the equilibrium. You'll finish by defending your work with a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning (CER) slide or panel. In Weeks 4–5 you'll add a pH & indicator diagram and a titration setup, then record a short narrated walkthrough.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Add new slides to the deck you started in Weeks 1–2: a Prediction slide (your committed guesses first), then one slide each for the three stresses (added reactant, pressure, temperature), each showing the shift direction, a slide noting a catalyst causes no shift, and a closing CER slide (claim, evidence, reasoning).
Option B — Hands-on (poster)
Add a stress panel to your poster: jot your committed predictions first, draw the three stresses with shift arrows and labels, add the “catalyst = no shift” note and a short CER panel (claim, evidence, reasoning), and photograph the updated poster into one document.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
The steps follow a scientist's arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. You commit a prediction before you diagram, then hold your work up against it.
1
OBSERVE. Pick a reversible reaction to use as your demo (for example, N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, or the N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2 demo). Note whether it is exothermic or endothermic, and count the gas moles on each side. Leave room — you'll add more in Weeks 4–5.
2
QUESTION & PREDICT — commit first. Before you diagram anything, ask: if I disturb this balanced system, which way will it move? On your deck (or in a corner of the poster) write a committed prediction for all three stresses now — added reactant, pressure increase, and temperature rise — one arrow each (left / right / no shift). Commit before you investigate; you'll check yourself against these later.
3
INVESTIGATE · Added reactant: draw the reaction with extra reactant added and an arrow showing the shift toward products. Label: “Add reactant → shifts right (consumes the added reactant).”
4
INVESTIGATE · Pressure: show the container squeezed smaller (higher pressure) and an arrow shifting toward the side with fewer moles of gas. Label: “Increase pressure → shifts toward fewer gas moles.”
5
INVESTIGATE · Temperature: write heat on the correct side (product if exothermic, reactant if endothermic), then draw an arrow for the shift when temperature rises. Label: “Heat is a reactant (endo) or product (exo) — treat it like a substance.”
6
ANALYZE. Set each shift beside your committed prediction from Step 2. Where they match, mark a check; where they differ, correct it and add one line naming what you missed (temperature and pressure trip people up most). Then add a summary note: “A catalyst speeds both directions equally — it does NOT shift the equilibrium.” Sign your name.
7
DEFEND — make a CER slide (or poster panel). Add a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning: Claim — a system at equilibrium counteracts a stress; Evidence — your three labeled shifts; Reasoning — tie it to the particles: a stress changes how often forward vs reverse events happen until the rates rebalance at a new equilibrium. (You'll speak this same CER aloud in the final Week-5 recording.)
8
EXTEND & SAVE. Save your updated file (deck or photo document). Keep it — you'll add the pH & indicator diagram and the titration setup in Weeks 4–5, then record a short narrated walkthrough.

☐

☐
