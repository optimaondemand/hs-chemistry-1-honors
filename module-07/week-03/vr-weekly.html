📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
This is Build 3 of 5 of your module-long Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station. Last week you built a reversible-reaction demo showing dynamic equilibrium — both arrows, equal rates, constant concentrations. This week you make it respond. You'll work like a scientist: observe your balanced demo, commit a prediction for how it will react before you build, then add a stress panel that shows the equilibrium shifting to partially counteract three stresses — an added reactant, a pressure change, and a temperature change — labeling each shift direction. You'll finish by defending your work with a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning (CER) card. This is the directional reasoning from Lesson 7.05, made big enough to point at and narrate. In Weeks 4–5 you'll add a pH & indicator station and a titration setup, then record the full walkthrough.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your Week-2 build still in place — the reversible-reaction equilibrium demo at Lab Bench A.
▶  Your notes from Lesson 7.05 (the three stresses and which way each shifts the equilibrium).
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Equilibrium & Acids Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: your Week-2 reversible-reaction demo is waiting at Lab Bench A. A new Whiteboard IFX shows this week's steps, a blank Prediction sticky, and three empty “stress” slots labeled Added Reactant, Pressure, and Temperature. A sticky note at your feet reads “Predict first, then make it respond.”
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
The steps follow a scientist's arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. You commit a prediction before you build, then hold your work up against it.
1
OBSERVE. Return to your Week-2 reversible-reaction demo. Confirm it still shows both arrows (forward and reverse), equal rates, and your “K = [products] / [reactants]” label — this is your baseline: a balanced system at rest. Add a stress panel next to it with three empty slots. Leave bench space to the right for the Week-4 pH & indicator station.
2
QUESTION & PREDICT — commit first. Before you change anything, ask: if I disturb this balanced system, which way will it move? On a “Prediction” sticky note, write a committed answer for all three stresses now — added reactant, pressure increase, and temperature rise — one arrow each (left / right / no shift). Commit before you investigate; you will check yourself against these later.
3
INVESTIGATE · Added-reactant slot: spawn extra reactant molecules into the demo and add a bold arrow showing the equilibrium shifting toward products. Sticky note: “Add reactant → shifts right (consumes the added reactant).”
4
INVESTIGATE · Pressure slot: use a piston or “shrink the box” prop to show increased pressure, and an arrow shifting toward the side with fewer moles of gas. Sticky note: “Increase pressure → shifts toward fewer gas moles.”
5
INVESTIGATE · Temperature slot: label your demo as exothermic or endothermic, write heat on the correct side (product if exothermic, reactant if endothermic), then add an arrow for the shift when temperature rises. Sticky note: “Heat is a reactant (endo) or product (exo) — treat it like a substance.”
6
ANALYZE. Set each observed shift beside your committed prediction from Step 2. Where they match, mark a check; where they differ, correct the note and add one line naming what you missed (temperature and pressure trip people up most). Then add a summary sticky note: “A catalyst speeds both directions equally — it does NOT shift the equilibrium.” Sign your name on it.
7
DEFEND — make a CER card. Place a Whiteboard IFX “CER” card on your station stating: Claim — a system at equilibrium counteracts a stress; Evidence — your three labeled shifts; Reasoning — tie it to the particles: a stress changes how often forward vs reverse events happen until the rates rebalance at a new equilibrium. (You will speak this same CER aloud in the final Week-5 recording.)
8
EXTEND & SAVE. Leave bench room to the right for the Week-4 pH & indicator station and Week-5 titration setup. Lock your stress panel, CER card, and labels so they aren't bumped, then take a snapshot and save it as EqAcid_Wk3_YourName — your project's saved progress for next week. Finally, take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows all three labeled stress shifts, your catalyst note, and your CER card. That screenshot is what you'll submit.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked work will be waiting when you return. Save the EqAcid_Wk3_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot showing the three labeled stress shifts (added reactant, pressure, temperature), your catalyst note, and your CER card to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write a one-sentence caption: name the three stresses and state which way the equilibrium shifts for each. (Your caption will be used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
3. Also in the comment box, add your CER reflection (2–3 sentences): Claim — what a stressed equilibrium does; Evidence — the shifts you built; Reasoning — why it happens, in terms of forward vs reverse rates.
