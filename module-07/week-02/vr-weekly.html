Reversible-Reaction Equilibrium Demo — Continue Your Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Reversible-Reaction Equilibrium Demo (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
This is Build 2 of 5 of the project you began last week: the Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station — the exhibit you'll narrate on camera at the end of the module. You return to the same persistent bench, where last week's entropy/spontaneity sort is still waiting. This week you add a Reversible-Reaction Equilibrium Demo: a forward-and-reverse reaction (for example, the colorless N2O4 ⇌ brown NO2 demo, or a generic A + B ⇌ C + D) built to show dynamic equilibrium — both arrows, equal rates, and concentrations that hold steady. You'll label K = [products]/[reactants] and note that a large K means products are favored. In the weeks ahead you'll add a Le Châtelier stress panel, a pH scale, and a titration station — then record the full walkthrough.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lesson 7.04 (dynamic equilibrium; K = [products]/[reactants]; large vs. small K).
▶  Your saved snapshot from last week, EqAcid_Wk1_YourName.
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Equilibrium & Acids Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: your Week 1 entropy/spontaneity sort is still on the bench, and a Whiteboard IFX now shows this week's Step 1 beside an empty zone marked Equilibrium Demo. A sticky note at your feet reads “Build 2 of 5 — start here.” You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 YOUR INVESTIGATION — STEP BY STEP
You'll move through a scientist's arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Commit a prediction before you build — then let your model test it.
1
OBSERVE. Confirm last week's entropy/spontaneity sort is still on the bench, then read the Whiteboard IFX. Recall the phenomenon from the lesson: a sealed reaction's color deepens, then holds perfectly steady — it looks finished, yet molecules are still reacting. Work in the Equilibrium Demo zone and leave open space to the right — a Le Châtelier stress panel (Week 3), a pH scale (Week 4), and a titration station (Week 5) are still to come.
2
QUESTION. Place a sticky note stating the driving question: “How does a reversible reaction reach balance — and why does it look still but isn't?” This is the question your build will answer.
3
PREDICT (commit this before building). On a second sticky note, write your answer now, before you place any molecules: “At equilibrium, will the reactant and product amounts be equal, or just unchanging? Will the reaction be stopped, or still running?” You'll check this prediction against your finished model in Step 6 — do not erase it.
4
INVESTIGATE — build the model. (a) Spawn a small group of reactant molecules on the left and product molecules on the right (use the N2O4 ⇌ 2 NO2 demo, or a generic A + B ⇌ C + D) and place a clear double arrow (⇌) between them. (b) Add two labeled rate arrows — forward and reverse — drawn the same length to show equal rates, with a sticky note: “Dynamic equilibrium: forward rate = reverse rate — concentrations stay constant, but the reaction never stops.” (c) Place a K label over the demo: K = [products] ÷ [reactants], and a note: “Large K → products favored; small K → reactants favored.”
5
ANALYZE. Step back and read your own model. Are both reactants and products still present — concentrations constant but not zero and not necessarily equal? Are the two rate arrows truly the same length? Then return to your Step 3 prediction and mark it right or wrong on the note, adding one line about what changed your mind (or confirmed it).
6
DEFEND (CER). Add one signed CER sticky note — see the CER panel below — that makes a Claim about what dynamic equilibrium is, cites Evidence from your model (equal-length arrows; both species still present; constant, non-zero amounts), and gives your Reasoning for why equal rates keep the amounts steady in a closed system.
7
EXTEND. On the K label, add a quick note on how the picture would change if K were very large versus very small, and leave clear bench space to the right for next week's Le Châtelier stress panel — a preview of what happens when you disturb this balance.
8
Lock & capture. Lock your demo and labels so they aren't bumped, take a snapshot and save it as EqAcid_Wk2_YourName (your saved progress for next week), then take one screenshot that clearly shows the double arrow, the equal-length rate arrows, the K = [products]/[reactants] label, your prediction note, and your signed CER note. That screenshot is what you'll submit.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked objects will be waiting when you return. Save the EqAcid_Wk2_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
✍️ YOUR CER REFLECTION (STEP 6)
Write these three parts on your signed sticky note (2–4 sentences total). You'll reuse this reasoning when you narrate the finished station on camera at the end of the module.
▶  Claim: State in one sentence what dynamic equilibrium is.
▶  Evidence: Point to what your model shows — the equal-length forward/reverse arrows, both species still present, and constant (non-zero, not necessarily equal) amounts.
▶  Reasoning: Explain why equal forward and reverse rates make the concentrations hold steady even though molecules keep converting — and why this needs a closed system.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot showing the reversible-reaction demo — double arrow, equal-length rate arrows, the K = [products]/[reactants] label, your committed prediction note (marked right/wrong), and your signed CER note — to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write your CER reflection (2–4 sentences: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning) defining dynamic equilibrium in your own words and stating what a large K tells you. (Your reflection will be used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
