📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER · THE INVESTIGATION ARC
Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Commit a prediction before you draw.
1
OBSERVE. Look back over your four earlier sections and picture the titration you're about to diagram: titrant dripping from a buret into an acid that holds a single drop of indicator, drop after drop, while almost nothing seems to change.
2
QUESTION. Fix the driving question: at the exact moment the base has neutralized the acid, what will the pH be — and how will you know you've arrived?
3
PREDICT — commit before you draw. On your slide or poster, write your committed prediction first: for this strong-acid / strong-base titration, what pH do you expect at the equivalence point, and what will the phenolphthalein do at that instant? Do this before you diagram anything — a prediction only counts if you make it in advance.
4
INVESTIGATE — diagram the setup. Diagram a titration setup: a buret of titrant above a flask of acid with a drop of indicator (phenolphthalein). Show the flask colorless in acid and pink once base is in excess; label: “colorless → pink (~pH 8.2–10).”
5
ANALYZE — mark the evidence and check your prediction. Mark the equivalence point and label it: “moles base = moles acid; strong–strong → pH 7; MaVa = MbVb.” Add a small pH-vs-volume curve sketch with the steep region marked. Then compare it to your Step-3 prediction: were you right? If not, note what you'd revise — a corrected prediction is a win, not a loss.
6
DEFEND — confirm and record. Confirm your deck/poster now has all five sections: (1) entropy & spontaneity, (2) dynamic equilibrium & K, (3) Le Châtelier shifts, (4) pH scale & indicators, (5) this titration; sign your name. Then record a narrated walkthrough (about 3–5 minutes) of all five sections, narrating and defending the chemistry of each with evidence. Use a screen recording (PowerPoint → Record) or a phone video of your poster.
7
EXTEND. Save your finished file, then submit the file + a one-sentence caption here; submit the narrated recording to the separate Module VR Project assignment. Then stretch your thinking: on your slide/poster or in your narration, answer “How would the equivalence-point pH change if the acid had been weak instead of strong — and why?”
✍️ CER REFLECTION (INCLUDE IN YOUR NARRATION OR ON YOUR SLIDE/POSTER)
Close your titration section by making a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning statement about the equivalence point:
Claim: State the equivalence-point pH for this strong-acid / strong-base titration.
Evidence: Name what you can actually observe that signals it — the indicator's color flip and the steep jump in the curve.
Reasoning: Explain why that pH occurs, using the salt that forms (Lesson 7.10) and MaVa = MbVb — and note how the claim would change for a weak acid.
