This week completes your five-week project. After you finish the Titration Station, you will record the full walkthrough video of your whole Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station. That recording is submitted to the separate, graded Module VR Project assignment — this weekly assignment still just asks for a completion screenshot + caption. Plan time to do both this week.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're adding the fifth and final station to your module-long Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station: a titration setup. You'll build a buret of titrant above a flask of acid with a drop of indicator, model the color change at the endpoint, and clearly mark the equivalence point where moles of base equal moles of acid. Then you'll record a narrated walkthrough of the entire station — entropy/spontaneity sort (Wk1), the reversible-reaction equilibrium demo (Wk2), the Le Châtelier shift panel (Wk3), the pH & indicator station (Wk4), and this titration (Wk5) — explaining the chemistry as you go.

▶  Your four earlier builds, already saved in the persistent Session.
▶  About 40–50 minutes in one sitting (building + recording).

📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER · THE INVESTIGATION ARC
Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Commit a prediction before you build.
1
OBSERVE. Walk your four earlier stations and read the Titration Station whiteboard vocabulary. Picture the setup you're about to model: titrant dripping from a buret into an acid that holds a single drop of indicator, drop after drop, while almost nothing seems to change.
2
QUESTION. Fix the driving question in your mind: at the exact moment the base has neutralized the acid, what will the pH be — and how will you know you've arrived?
3
PREDICT — commit before you build. Spawn a sticky note first and write your committed prediction: for this strong-acid / strong-base titration, what pH do you expect at the equivalence point, and what will the phenolphthalein do at that instant? Place the note where the camera will catch it. Do this before you build anything — a prediction only counts if you make it in advance.
4
INVESTIGATE — build the setup. In the Titration Station zone, spawn a buret (the tall measuring tube of titrant) above a flask of acid, and add a labeled drop of indicator (phenolphthalein). Model the color change: colorless while acidic, turning pink once base is in excess. Add a sticky note: “Phenolphthalein: colorless in acid → pink in base (~pH 8.2–10).”
5
ANALYZE — mark the evidence and check your prediction. Place a labeled marker (or a small pH-vs-volume curve sketch with the 3D pen) at the equivalence point and a sticky note: “Equivalence point: moles base = moles acid. Strong–strong → pH 7. MaVa = MbVb.” Now compare it to your Step-3 prediction: were you right? If not, add a quick note on what you'd revise — a corrected prediction is a win, not a loss.
6
DEFEND — lock, snapshot, and record. Lock the titration station and its labels, then take a snapshot saved as EqAcid_Final_YourName (Build 5 of 5 — your finished project's saved state). Then move to the Lab Entrance spawn point and record the walkthrough video: walk past all five stations in order, narrating and defending the chemistry of each with evidence — spontaneity/entropy, dynamic equilibrium & K, Le Châtelier shifts, the pH scale & indicators, and this titration with its equivalence point. Aim for about 3–5 minutes.
7
EXTEND. Take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows your finished Titration Station with its equivalence-point label — that screenshot is what you submit here (the recorded video goes to the separate Module VR Project). Then stretch your thinking: on your closing sticky note or in your narration, answer “How would the equivalence-point pH change if the acid had been weak instead of strong — and why?”
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked stations will be waiting when you return. Save the EqAcid_Final_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
✍️ CER REFLECTION (INCLUDE IN YOUR NARRATION OR ON A CLOSING STICKY NOTE)
Close your titration station by making a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning statement about the equivalence point:
Claim: State the equivalence-point pH for this strong-acid / strong-base titration.
Evidence: Name what you can actually observe that signals it — the indicator's color flip and the steep jump in the curve.
Reasoning: Explain why that pH occurs, using the salt that forms (Lesson 7.10) and MaVa = MbVb — and note how the claim would change for a weak acid.
