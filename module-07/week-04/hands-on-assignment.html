📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
From this week's lessons: proton transfer and conjugate pairs (7.07), the pH/pOH conversions and the pH + pOH = 14 rule (7.08), and strong-vs-weak strength with Ka (7.09). Here you'll do the calculations by hand — the real fluency every later acid-base problem depends on.
This is your week's “doing chemistry” work. Show your steps, then check yourself against the key.
🔢 FORMULAS & REFERENCE YOU'LL NEED
pH = −log[H3O+]  ·  pOH = −log[OH−]  ·  [H3O+] = 10−pH
pH + pOH = 14.00 (at 25 °C)  ·  Kw = [H3O+][OH−] = 1.0 × 10−14 (25 °C)
Neutral pH = 7  ·  acidic < 7  ·  basic > 7.   Round pH/pOH answers to two decimal places.

🧪 THE PROBLEMS (8 ITEMS)
1. A solution has [H3O+] = 1.0 × 10−4 M. Find its pH, and state whether it is acidic, basic, or neutral.
2. A solution has [H3O+] = 1.0 × 10−11 M. Find its pH, and classify it.
3. A solution has a pH of 2.00. Find its [H3O+] (use [H3O+] = 10−pH).
4. A solution has a pH of 8.00. Find its pOH and its [OH−].
5. A cleaning solution has [OH−] = 1.0 × 10−3 M. Find its pOH, then its pH, and classify it.
6. Classify each as acidic, basic, or neutral: (a) pH 6.50, (b) pH 7.00, (c) pH 12.30, (d) pH 4.00.
7. For the reaction HF + H2O ⇌ H3O+ + F−, name both conjugate acid-base pairs and identify which species is the Brønsted-Lowry acid.
8. Three acids have Ka values of 1 × 10−2, 1.8 × 10−5, and 4 × 10−10. Rank them from strongest to weakest acid, and explain in one sentence how Ka tells you.
▶  Check your work — answer key
1. pH = −log(1.0 × 10−4) = 4.00. Below 7, so acidic.
2. pH = −log(1.0 × 10−11) = 11.00. Above 7, so basic.
3. [H3O+] = 10−2.00 = 1.0 × 10−2 M (0.010 M).
4. pOH = 14.00 − 8.00 = 6.00; [OH−] = 10−6.00 = 1.0 × 10−6 M.
5. pOH = −log(1.0 × 10−3) = 3.00; pH = 14.00 − 3.00 = 11.00. Above 7, so basic.
6. (a) pH 6.50 → acidic; (b) pH 7.00 → neutral; (c) pH 12.30 → basic; (d) pH 4.00 → acidic.
7. HF is the Brønsted-Lowry acid (it donates a proton); its conjugate base is F− (the HF / F− pair). H2O is the base; its conjugate acid is H3O+ (the H2O / H3O+ pair). Each pair differs by exactly one H+.
8. Strongest → weakest: 1 × 10−2 > 1.8 × 10−5 > 4 × 10−10. A larger Ka means the ionization lies further toward products — more protons donated — so it marks the stronger acid.
✍️ EXPLAIN YOUR REASONING (SELF-CHECK)
Before you check the key, write two or three sentences in your own words answering the question below. This is part of your self-check — there is no separate point value; it is folded into the “self-check & corrections” credit already in the rubric.
Two solutions are each 0.10 M — one is HCl, the other is acetic acid. Which has the lower pH, and why? Explain in terms of how completely each acid ionizes, not just “it's stronger.”
▶  Reveal a model answer
HCl has the lower pH. Both solutions are equally concentrated (0.10 M), so the difference is not how much acid is dissolved. HCl is a strong acid: it ionizes essentially completely, so nearly all of it releases H3O+, giving [H3O+] ≈ 0.10 M and pH ≈ 1.0. Acetic acid is weak: it ionizes only partially (Ka ≈ 1.8 × 10−5), so only a small fraction of its molecules donate a proton, leaving a much lower [H3O+] and a higher pH (about 2.9). The gap comes entirely from how completely each ionizes — strength, not concentration.
