📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're adding the pH & Indicator Station — Build 4 of 5 — to your growing Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station project, using a PowerPoint deck or a hand-drawn poster. This week you build a labeled pH scale from 0 to 14, place strong and weak acids and a strong base where they belong, add indicator color swatches, and annotate the two governing relationships: pH = −log[H3O+] and pH + pOH = 14. Keep the file you've been adding to since Week 1 — next week (Build 5) you'll add a titration setup and record a short narrated walkthrough.
👀 OBSERVE → QUESTION → PREDICT · DO THIS BEFORE YOU BUILD
Observe. From this week's lessons: HCl and acetic acid are both acids, yet at the same concentration HCl drives pH far lower. Question. Where should a strong acid, a weak acid, and a strong base each land on a 0–14 pH scale — and why do a strong and a weak acid at equal concentration sit at different pH?
Predict first — commit before you build. In your notebook (or the Canvas comment box) write one sentence predicting: will a strong acid or a weak acid of the same concentration sit lower on the pH scale, and what does a lower pH say about [H3O+]? Lock this prediction in now — do not revise it later. You'll test it against the scale you build.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Add a new slide to your project deck: a horizontal pH scale (0–14) with neutral 7 marked; place a strong acid, a weak acid, and a strong base with labels; add indicator color swatches; and a text box with the two relationships.
Option B — Hands-on (poster)
Draw the labeled pH scale on paper with colored bands for the indicator, place and label the acids/base, write the two relationships underneath, and photograph it into your project document.
📝 INVESTIGATE · BUILD IT, IN ORDER
1
Draw a horizontal pH scale from 0 to 14. Mark the midpoint 7 and label: “7 = neutral; below 7 acidic; above 7 basic (25 °C).”
2
Place one strong acid (e.g., HCl) low on the scale, labeled “strong — ionizes ~100%,” and one weak acid (e.g., acetic acid) higher up, labeled “weak — ionizes partially (Ka ≈ 1.8 × 10−5).”
3
Place one strong base (e.g., NaOH) high on the scale, labeled “strong base — dissociates completely.”
4
Add indicator color swatches across the scale (e.g., one color in the acidic region, another in the basic region). Label one with an indicator you know, such as “phenolphthalein: colorless in acid, pink in base.”
5
Add a relationships note: “pH = −log[H3O+]” and “pH + pOH = 14 (25 °C); Kw = 1.0 × 10−14.” Sign your name.
6
Save your file (deck or photo document). Keep it — you'll add the titration setup and record the walkthrough in Week 5 (Build 5 of 5).
🔬 ANALYZE → DEFEND → EXTEND
Analyze. Stand back and look at your finished scale. Did the strong acid land lower than the weak acid? Compare where each landed against the prediction you committed to before building — and note whether your prediction held.
Defend — write a short CER reflection (3–4 sentences; you'll submit this with your work):
• Claim: which acid on your scale is more acidic, and was your committed prediction right?
• Evidence: the pH positions and labels on your scale, and what a lower pH means about [H3O+].
• Reasoning: tie it to strong vs. weak — explain why complete vs. partial ionization, not concentration, produces the gap.
Extend. Carbonic acid forms when CO2 dissolves in seawater — a weak acid slowly lowering ocean pH. Where would it sit on your scale, and would you expect it to produce a large or a small pH change? (Next week, Build 5, you'll add a titration setup and record a short narrated walkthrough.)
