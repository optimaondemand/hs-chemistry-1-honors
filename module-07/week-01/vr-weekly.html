Week 1 · OptimaXR
Entropy & Spontaneity Sort — Begin Your Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Entropy & Spontaneity Sort (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You're starting the project you'll build across all five weeks of this module: the Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station — a lab exhibit that you'll narrate on camera at the end of the module. This week you build the foundation: an Entropy & Spontaneity Sort. You'll sort a set of sample processes into more-entropy vs. less-entropy bins, and into spontaneous vs. nonspontaneous bins, labeling why for each (gas formed, dissolving, temperature up, more gas moles). One anchor sticky note — “ΔSuniv > 0 for a spontaneous process” — will sit at the heart of the station all module. In later weeks you'll add an equilibrium demo (Week 2), a Le Châtelier shift panel (Week 3), a pH & indicator station (Week 4), and a titration setup (Week 5) — and finally record the full walkthrough.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lessons 7.01–7.02 (the ΔS sign rules; spontaneity is direction not speed; ΔSuniv > 0).
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module — join Equilibrium & Acids Lab
Location: Equilibrium & Acids Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: a Whiteboard IFX facing the bench shows this week's Step 1 and the anchor rule ΔSuniv > 0 for a spontaneous process, beside four empty labeled bins — More Entropy, Less Entropy, Spontaneous, Nonspontaneous — and a tray of process cards to sort. A sticky note at your feet reads “Start here.” You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER · OBSERVE → QUESTION → PREDICT → INVESTIGATE → ANALYZE → DEFEND → EXTEND
1
Observe. At the bench, read the Whiteboard IFX and the anchor rule, then look over the tray of process cards and the four empty bins — More Entropy, Less Entropy, Spontaneous, Nonspontaneous. Leave open space along the bench to the right — you'll add an equilibrium demo (Week 2), a Le Châtelier panel (Week 3), a pH station (Week 4), and a titration setup (Week 5).
2
Question. On a fresh sticky note, write the question you're investigating and post it above the bins: “Which of these changes happen on their own — and does ‘more entropy’ always mean ‘spontaneous’?”
3
Predict — commit before you build. Before you sort a single card, take a Prediction sticky note and commit in writing: pick salt dissolving and 2 NO2(g) → N2O4(g), predict which entropy bin and which spontaneity bin each will land in, and why. Post it face-up and do not change it — you'll test it against your results.
4
Investigate. Pick up the process cards (for example: ice melting, steam condensing, salt dissolving, 2 NO2(g) → N2O4(g), a hot drink cooling to room temperature, rust un-rusting back into iron). Drop each into the correct entropy bin (ΔS > 0 or ΔS < 0) and then the correct spontaneity bin, adding a short reason note to each (e.g. “gas formed,” “dissolving,” “temperature up,” “more gas moles.”) Reminder: rust un-rusting and a cool drink reheating itself are nonspontaneous; the rest run on their own.
5
Analyze. Compare your sort to your Prediction note. Which card, if any, surprised you? Flag the one that lowers the system's entropy or absorbs heat yet is still spontaneous — that's the case only the total-entropy rule can explain.
6
Defend. Place the anchor summary sticky note at the center of the station — “ΔSuniv > 0 for a spontaneous process” — and sign your name. This is the rule that justifies every placement you made.
7
Extend & save. Lock your bins, cards, and labels so they aren't bumped; take a snapshot saved as EqAcid_Wk1_YourName (your project's saved progress for next week); and take one screenshot that clearly shows both sets of sorted bins, the reason notes, and your “ΔSuniv > 0” anchor note. That screenshot is what you'll submit.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked work will be waiting when you return. Save the EqAcid_Wk1_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
✍️ FINISH WITH A CER REFLECTION
Before you log out, write three short sentences about the process that surprised you most (the one you flagged in the Analyze step):
• Claim — is it spontaneous, and does the system's entropy rise or fall?
• Evidence — what did you notice as you sorted it (gas moles, phase change, heat taken in or given off)?
• Reasoning — tie it to the anchor rule: how does ΔSuniv > 0 explain why it happens on its own even if the system's own entropy fell?
You'll paste this CER into the submission comment box.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot showing the sorted entropy and spontaneity bins, the reason notes, your committed Prediction note, and your “ΔSuniv > 0” anchor note to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, paste your three-sentence CER reflection (Claim, Evidence, Reasoning) about the process that surprised you most. (Its opening line doubles as your caption when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
