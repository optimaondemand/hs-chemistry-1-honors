Week 1 · No-Headset Pathway
Entropy & Spontaneity Sort
Assignment · 20 pts · ~35–45 min
✔️ THIS IS THE ALTERNATE
This pathway earns the same credit and meets the same objectives as the OptimaXR version. Complete this OR the VR assignment — not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You'll build an Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station — a project built up across five weeks that you'll explain at the end of the module — using a PowerPoint deck or a hand-drawn poster. This week you build the foundation: an Entropy & Spontaneity Sort. You'll sort a set of sample processes into more-entropy vs. less-entropy groups, and into spontaneous vs. nonspontaneous groups, labeling why for each, with one anchor statement: “ΔSuniv > 0 for a spontaneous process.” In later weeks you'll add an equilibrium demo (Week 2), a Le Châtelier panel (Week 3), a pH station (Week 4), and a titration diagram (Week 5), then record a short narrated walkthrough.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Make a short deck: a title slide with the question; a Prediction slide (committed before sorting); a slide sorting the processes into more entropy / less entropy with reasons; a slide sorting them into spontaneous / nonspontaneous with reasons; a slide stating the anchor rule; and a closing CER reflection slide.
Option B — Hands-on (poster)
On paper, write the question and a Prediction box (committed before sorting), draw four labeled boxes (more entropy, less entropy, spontaneous, nonspontaneous), write each process in the right box with its reason, add the anchor rule and a CER reflection box, and photograph the poster into one document.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER · OBSERVE → QUESTION → PREDICT → INVESTIGATE → ANALYZE → DEFEND → EXTEND
1
Observe. Set up your deck or poster: title it Equilibrium & Acid-Base Station, create four labeled areas — more entropy, less entropy, spontaneous, nonspontaneous — and read over the six processes: ice melting, steam condensing, salt dissolving in water, 2 NO2(g) → N2O4(g), a hot drink cooling to room temperature, and rust un-rusting back into shiny iron. Leave room — you'll add more sections in Weeks 2–5.
2
Question. On your title slide or at the top of your poster, write the question you're investigating: “Which of these changes happen on their own — and does ‘more entropy’ always mean ‘spontaneous’?”
3
Predict — commit before you build. Before you sort anything, write a Prediction box: pick salt dissolving and 2 NO2(g) → N2O4(g), predict which entropy group and which spontaneity group each will land in, and why. Do not erase or change it — you'll test it against your results.
4
Investigate. Sort each process into more entropy (ΔS > 0) or less entropy (ΔS < 0) with a short reason (e.g. “gas formed,” “dissolving,” “fewer gas moles”), then sort the same processes into spontaneous or nonspontaneous with a one-line reason. (Reminder: rust un-rusting and a cool drink reheating itself are nonspontaneous.)
5
Analyze. Compare your sort to your Prediction box. Which process, if any, surprised you? Flag the one that lowers the system's entropy or absorbs heat yet is still spontaneous — that's the case only the total-entropy rule can explain.
6
Defend. Add the anchor statement at the center — “ΔSuniv > 0 for a spontaneous process” — and sign your name. This is the rule that justifies every placement you made.
7
Extend & save. Save your finished file (deck or photo document). Keep it — you'll add the equilibrium, Le Châtelier, pH, and titration sections in Weeks 2–5.
✍️ FINISH WITH A CER REFLECTION
Add a final slide (or a box on your poster) with three short sentences about the process that surprised you most (the one you flagged in the Analyze step):
• Claim — is it spontaneous, and does the system's entropy rise or fall?
• Evidence — what did you notice as you sorted it (gas moles, phase change, heat taken in or given off)?
• Reasoning — tie it to the anchor rule: how does ΔSuniv > 0 explain why it happens on its own even if the system's own entropy fell?
You'll also paste this CER into the submission comment box.
