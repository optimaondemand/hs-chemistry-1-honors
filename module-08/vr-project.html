📚 THE BIG PICTURE
Across this module you've built an Electrochemical Cell & Rate Build in OptimaXR, one week at a time, to answer a single running question: how do chemists turn chemistry into electricity, predict whether a cell will run on its own, and control how fast a reaction goes? Week 1 you built a galvanic cell foundation; Week 2 a cell-potential panel; Week 3 a real cells & electrolysis panel; Week 4 a reaction-rate & catalysis demo. Now you bring it all together.
Your task is to record a guided video walkthrough inside the headset — a short tour in which you narrate the chemistry of your build, from spontaneous redox in a galvanic cell, through cell potential and free energy, to electrolysis, and on to what makes a reaction fast or slow. This recorded explanation, not a static screenshot, is what earns the grade. The build is the stage; your spoken reasoning is the performance.

This walkthrough is one of only two teacher-reviewed pieces in Module 8 (the other is your Battery Argument writing assignment). Your teacher will watch your recording and respond to your reasoning personally — so make it your best thinking, clearly explained.

🎤 WHAT YOUR RECORDING MUST COVER (aim for 3–5 minutes)
1
Open at the entrance. Introduce your build and state the big question it answers: how do chemists turn chemistry into electricity, predict whether a cell will run, and control how fast a reaction goes?
2
Galvanic cell & redox (Week 1). Walk your galvanic cell. Explain that a galvanic cell runs a spontaneous redox reaction to make electricity, that oxidation is loss of electrons at the anode (the negative terminal) and reduction is gain of electrons at the cathode (the positive terminal), and that electrons flow anode → cathode through the external wire while the salt bridge keeps each half-cell neutral. Use the Daniell cell: Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu.
3
Cell potential, free energy & equilibrium (Week 2). At your cell-potential panel, read the voltmeter (Zn–Cu ≈ +1.10 V) and explain E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. State the through-line: a positive E°cell means a spontaneous (galvanic) cell, and ΔG° = −nFE°cell — so E°cell > 0 gives ΔG° < 0 and K > 1 (products favored).
4
Real cells, corrosion & electrolysis (Week 3). Use your real-cells & electrolysis panel to contrast a primary (single-use) cell with a secondary (rechargeable) cell, point out an H2–O2 fuel cell, show the corrosion / sacrificial-anode demo, and then explain the key reversal: an electrolytic cell uses outside power to drive a non-spontaneous reaction (E°cell < 0). Galvanic makes electricity; electrolytic uses it.
5
Rates & catalysis (Week 4). At your reaction-rate & catalysis demo, walk the energy-profile diagram: a reaction needs an activation energy Ea to proceed, and a catalyst lowers Ea by offering an alternate pathway without being used up. Name the factors that raise rate (concentration, temperature, surface area, catalyst, nature of reactants) and state collision theory: collisions must have enough energy and the right orientation.
6
Close with the evidence — this is required. Before your final through-line, justify the chemistry with the evidence: explain why the galvanic cell is spontaneous (E°cell > 0, so ΔG° = −nFE°cell < 0), how an electrolytic cell differs (it must be driven by outside power, E°cell < 0), and how a catalyst speeds a reaction (it lowers Ea by an alternate pathway without being used up). Then land the through-line: thermodynamics tells us the direction — whether a cell runs on its own — while kinetics tells us the speed; together they let chemists both predict and control chemical change.
How to record: Use OptimaXR's video recording feature to capture your narrated tour from inside the headset (start the recording from the menu/tablet, walk and talk, then stop and save). If your setup can't capture in-headset audio, record your screen with narration instead. Aim for one clean take; a second take is fine.
