📚 THE BIG PICTURE
Across this module you've assembled an Electrochemical Cell & Rate Build — on slides or as physical models — one week at a time: Week 1 a galvanic cell foundation; Week 2 a cell-potential panel; Week 3 a real cells & electrolysis panel; Week 4 a reaction-rate & catalysis demo.
Now you record a narrated walkthrough of your build. The graded work is your spoken explanation of the chemistry, not the artwork — the build is the stage; your reasoning is the performance.
⭐ WHY THIS MATTERS
This walkthrough is one of only two teacher-reviewed pieces in Module 8 (the other is your Battery Argument writing assignment). Your teacher will watch your recording and respond to your reasoning personally.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (screen-recorded slides)
Use your four weekly PowerPoint builds as the visual. Record your screen while you narrate a tour: the galvanic cell foundation, the cell-potential panel, the real-cells & electrolysis panel, and the reaction-rate & catalysis demo.
Option B — Hands-on (physical models)
Use a drawn galvanic cell (two beakers, salt bridge, labeled anode/cathode and electron arrows); a voltmeter card with a standard-reduction-potential ranking strip; labeled cards for a primary cell, a secondary cell, a fuel cell, a corrosion/sacrificial-anode demo, and an electrolytic cell; and a hand-drawn energy-profile diagram with Ea and a lower catalyzed curve. Record a video narrating the same tour.
🎤 WHAT YOUR RECORDING MUST COVER (aim for 3–5 minutes)
1
Open with the big question: how do chemists turn chemistry into electricity, predict whether a cell will run, and control how fast a reaction goes?
2
Galvanic cell & redox (Week 1). Walk your cell. Explain that a galvanic cell runs a spontaneous redox reaction to make electricity, that oxidation = loss of electrons at the anode (negative terminal) and reduction = gain of electrons at the cathode (positive terminal), and that electrons flow anode → cathode through the wire while the salt bridge keeps neutrality. Use the Daniell cell: Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu.
3
Cell potential, free energy & equilibrium (Week 2). Show the voltmeter (Zn–Cu ≈ +1.10 V) and explain E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. State the through-line: a positive E°cell means a spontaneous cell, and ΔG° = −nFE°cell — so E°cell > 0 gives ΔG° < 0 and K > 1.
4
Real cells, corrosion & electrolysis (Week 3). Contrast a primary (single-use) cell with a secondary (rechargeable) cell, point out an H2–O2 fuel cell, show the corrosion / sacrificial-anode demo, and explain the reversal: an electrolytic cell uses outside power to drive a non-spontaneous reaction (E°cell < 0). Galvanic makes electricity; electrolytic uses it.
5
Rates & catalysis (Week 4). Walk the energy-profile diagram: a reaction needs an activation energy Ea, and a catalyst lowers Ea by an alternate pathway without being used up. Name the rate factors (concentration, temperature, surface area, catalyst, nature of reactants) and state collision theory: collisions need enough energy and the right orientation.
6
Close with the evidence — this is required. Before your final through-line, justify the chemistry with the evidence: explain why the galvanic cell is spontaneous (E°cell > 0, so ΔG° = −nFE°cell < 0), how an electrolytic cell differs (it must be driven by outside power, E°cell < 0), and how a catalyst speeds a reaction (it lowers Ea by an alternate pathway without being used up). Then land the through-line: thermodynamics tells us the direction — whether a cell runs on its own — while kinetics tells us the speed; together they let chemists both predict and control chemical change.
