Cell Potential Panel — Continue Your Electrochemical Cell & Rate Build
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Cell Potential Panel (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
This is Build 2 of 4 of the project you began last week: the Electrochemical Cell & Rate Build — the exhibit you'll narrate on camera at the end of the module. You return to the same persistent bench, where last week's galvanic cell (anode −, cathode +, salt bridge, electrons flowing anode → cathode) is still waiting. This week you add a Cell Potential Panel: a voltmeter that reads the cell's E°cell (about +1.10 V for the Zn–Cu cell), and a standard-reduction-potential ranking strip that orders several half-reactions from most-positive at the top (the strongest oxidizer, which becomes the cathode) down to the most-negative. You'll label the rule E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode and sign a sticky note carrying the module's through-line. In the weeks ahead you'll add real cells & electrolysis (Week 3) and a rate & catalysis demo (Week 4) — then record the full walkthrough.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lessons 8.03–8.04 (the reduction-potential table; E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; ΔG° = −nFE°).
▶  Your saved snapshot from last week, ElectroRate_Wk1_YourName.
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session: “Electrochem & Rates Lab” — see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: your Week 1 galvanic cell is still on the bench, and a Whiteboard IFX now shows this week's Step 1 beside an empty zone marked Cell Potential Panel. A sticky note at your feet reads “Build 2 of 4 — start here.” You won't have to guess what to do.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
This build follows an investigation arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend. Commit your prediction before you measure.
1
Observe. Confirm last week's galvanic cell is still on the bench, then read the Whiteboard IFX. Note the empty Cell Potential Panel zone, and leave open space to the right — you'll add real cells & electrolysis (Week 3) and a rate & catalysis demo (Week 4).
2
Question. Stand at the cell and pose the week's question, out loud or on a sticky note: Which metal is the cathode, and exactly how many volts should the meter read? You'll answer it by prediction first, then by measurement.
3
Predict (commit first). Before you spawn the voltmeter, commit a prediction on a sticky note: name which half-reaction is the cathode (the higher E°) and the value of E°cell you expect from E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. Commit to a number before you measure — that is the whole point of the panel.
4
Investigate. Now spawn a voltmeter and connect it across your Zn–Cu cell; set its display to read E°cell = +1.10 V. Then build a vertical standard-reduction-potential ranking strip, stacking half-reaction tiles from most-positive E° at the TOP (strongest oxidizer / cathode) to most-negative at the bottom: Ag+/Ag (+0.80) → Cu2+/Cu (+0.34) → 2H+/H2 (0.00, SHE) → Zn2+/Zn (−0.76).
5
Analyze. Place the rule label E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode over the panel and use the 3D pen to draw arrows from the Cu tile (cathode) and the Zn tile (anode) to the voltmeter, showing where +1.10 V comes from. Compare the +1.10 V result to your committed prediction — did the cathode you chose and the number you expected match?
6
Defend. Place and sign a sticky note with the module's through-line: “E°cell > 0 → spontaneous → ΔG° = −nFE° < 0.” In your own words, add one line arguing why a positive voltage and a negative free-energy change say the same thing.
7
Extend. Lock your panel and labels so they aren't bumped, then take a snapshot saved as ElectroRate_Wk2_YourName (your saved progress for next week) and one screenshot that clearly shows the voltmeter at +1.10 V, the ranking strip (most-positive on top), the E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode label, and your signed through-line note. That screenshot is what you'll submit — and the open bench to the right is where Weeks 3–4 will grow.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked objects will be waiting when you return. Save the ElectroRate_Wk2_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost.
📝 CER REFLECTION · DEFEND YOUR PANEL
Before you leave the headset, write a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning statement about your Zn–Cu cell (3–4 sentences, in your notebook or the Canvas comment box):
▶  Claim: Is the Zn–Cu cell spontaneous (galvanic), or not?
▶  Evidence: Cite the voltmeter reading and the two half-reactions' E° values from your ranking strip.
▶  Reasoning: Use E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode and the link ΔG° = −nFE° to explain why that evidence supports your claim — and note whether your Step-3 prediction held up.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot showing the Cell Potential Panel — the voltmeter reading +1.10 V, the ranking strip ordered most-positive on top, the E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode label, and your signed through-line note — to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, post your CER reflection (Claim–Evidence–Reasoning) from above. Its first line doubles as the one-sentence caption used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.
