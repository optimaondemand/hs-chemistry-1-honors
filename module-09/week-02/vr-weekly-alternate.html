Week 2 · No-Headset Pathway
Build a Half-Life & Energy Station
Assignment · 20 pts · ~35–45 min
✔️ THIS IS THE ALTERNATE
This pathway earns the same credit and meets the same objectives as the OptimaXR version. Complete this OR the VR assignment — not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You'll build a Half-Life & Energy Station — two displays — using a PowerPoint deck or drawn/physical models photographed at home. Display 1 is a half-life decay model showing a sample halving across successive half-lives (1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8…). Display 2 is a fission-vs-fusion comparison panel (U-235 chain reaction vs. D–T fusion). This is the second of three weekly builds; next week you'll add a radioisotope applications exhibit and record a short narrated walkthrough.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Make a short deck: a title slide, one slide for the half-life decay model (show the row of objects halving step by step), and one slide for the fission-vs-fusion panel (two columns).
Option B — Hands-on (drawn / physical)
Use coins, beans, or paper tokens to model a sample halving across half-lives, and draw the fission/fusion panel on paper. Photograph each display and combine the photos into one document.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
Work the investigator's arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend.
1
Observe. Look back at your Week 1 no-headset build (your decay & stability display) if you have it. Recall the phenomenon: a single nucleus is unpredictable, yet a whole sample decays on a steady schedule.
2
Question. Hold this week's driving question in mind as you build: How much of a sample is left after several half-lives — and where does the enormous energy of fission and fusion actually come from?
3
Predict — commit before you build. At the top of your first slide or page, write two committed predictions: (a) “After 4 half-lives, about ______ of the sample remains,” and (b) “Per gram of fuel, I think ______ (fission or fusion) releases more energy.” Lock in your answers now — you will test them against your finished displays.
4
Investigate — build both displays. Build the half-life decay model: start with a set amount (say 16 or 32 tokens) and show half remaining at each step — start, after 1 half-life, after 2, after 3… The amounts should read 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8…, each step labeled with its fraction. Then build the fission-vs-fusion comparison panel: on one side show the U-235 chain reaction (heavy nucleus splits into fragments + neutrons that split more); on the other show D–T fusion (21H + 31H → 42He + 10n). Use drawings or labeled equations.
5
Analyze. Read your finished displays. Does the decay model show a true halving (half of what remains each step) rather than removing the same number each time? Compare your displays to your Step 3 predictions — where were you right, and what surprised you?
6
Defend. Add two caption labels (your “sticky notes”): one on the decay model reading “after n half-lives, (1/2)n remains”; one on the comparison panel reading “fission splits, fusion combines; both release energy.”
7
Extend. Save your finished station (deck or photo document) — keep it tidy, since you'll add the radioisotope applications exhibit in Week 3 and record a narrated walkthrough. Then name one real place this chemistry shows up — a medical scan, a power reactor, or the Sun itself.
