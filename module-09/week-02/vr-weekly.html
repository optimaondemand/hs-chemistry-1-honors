Week 2 · OptimaXR
Build Your Half-Life & Energy Station
VR Assignment · 20 pts · one headset session (~35–45 min)
🧘 PREFER NOT TO USE A HEADSET?
A full non-VR alternate — “Build a Half-Life & Energy Station (No-Headset Pathway)” — meets the exact same objectives and earns the same credit. Do one pathway, not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
This is Build 2 of 3 of your cumulative Nuclear Decay & Applications Exhibit. Returning to the same Nuclear Science Lab where last week's decay & stability model is waiting, you'll add a Half-Life & Energy Station: a half-life decay model (a sample visibly halving across successive half-lives) and a fission-vs-fusion comparison panel (U-235 chain reaction vs. D–T fusion). Next week you'll add the radioisotope applications exhibit and record your narrated walkthrough — so leave room.
🥽 WHAT YOU'LL NEED
▶  A VR headset with the OptimaXR app (or the OptimaXR desktop app).
▶  Your notes from Lessons 9.03 and 9.04 (the (1/2)n pattern; fission vs. fusion).
▶  Your Nuclear_Wk1_YourName snapshot from last week.
▶  About 35–45 minutes in one sitting.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset (or OptimaXR desktop app)
Session type: persistent (your placed objects stay between visits all module)
Session ID: see the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list in this module
Location: Nuclear Science Lab
Spawn point: Lab Bench A
When you arrive: last week's decay & stability model is still on the bench (the Session is persistent). Open your Nuclear_Wk1_YourName snapshot if anything looks out of place, then build this week's station beside it.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
Work the investigator's arc: Observe → Question → Predict → Investigate → Analyze → Defend → Extend.
1
Observe. Arrive at the Nuclear Science Lab (spawn Lab Bench A). Last week's decay & stability model is still on the bench — open your Nuclear_Wk1_YourName snapshot if anything looks out of place. Watch how an unstable nucleus changes, and notice the tension: a single nucleus is unpredictable, yet a whole sample decays on a steady schedule.
2
Question. Hold this week's driving question in mind as you build: How much of a sample is left after several half-lives — and where does the enormous energy of fission and fusion actually come from?
3
Predict — commit before you build. Spawn and sign a sticky note with two committed predictions: (a) “After 4 half-lives, about ______ of the sample remains,” and (b) “Per gram of fuel, I think ______ (fission or fusion) releases more energy.” Lock in your answers now — you will test them against your finished model.
4
Investigate — build the station. Beside last week's model, build a half-life decay model: spawn a row of identical objects (say 16 or 32 small spheres) shown at a short series of stations — start, after 1 half-life, after 2, after 3… — each showing half of the previous amount (remove or grey out the rest), so the row reads 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8… Then build a fission-vs-fusion comparison panel: the U-235 chain reaction (a heavy nucleus splitting into fragments + neutrons that go on to split more) on one side, and D–T fusion (21H + 31H → 42He + 10n) on the other. Use spawned objects, the 3D pen, or labeled equations — whatever reads clearly.
5
Analyze. Step back and read your model. Does the decay row show a true halving (half of what remains each step) rather than removing the same number each time? Compare the finished station to your Step 3 predictions — where were you right, and what surprised you?
6
Defend. Place and sign two sticky notes: one on the decay model reading “after n half-lives, (1/2)n remains”; one on the comparison panel reading “fission splits, fusion combines; both release energy.” Lock your new objects so they aren't bumped, then take a snapshot and save it as Nuclear_Wk2_YourName — your project's saved progress for next week.
7
Extend. Take one screenshot inside the headset that clearly shows your half-life decay model and your fission-vs-fusion panel together (this is what you'll submit). Then look forward: leave bench room for Week 3's radioisotope applications exhibit, and name one real place this chemistry shows up — a medical scan, a power reactor, or the Sun itself.
Need to stop partway? Because the Session is persistent, your placed and locked objects will be waiting when you return. Save the Nuclear_Wk2_YourName snapshot before you log out so nothing is lost. Leave bench room for next week's radioisotope applications exhibit.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your in-headset screenshot of the Half-Life & Energy Station (decay model + fission-vs-fusion panel) to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write a short CER reflection (3–4 sentences): Claim — why do both fission and fusion release energy? Evidence — point to what your built model shows (the halving row and/or the split-vs-combine panel). Reasoning — connect it to the (1/2)n pattern and to mass converting to energy (E = mc2) as products move toward the iron peak. (Your reflection will be used when your work appears in the class gallery walk later this module.)
