Week 1 · No-Headset Pathway
Build a Nuclear Decay & Stability Model
Assignment · 20 pts · ~35–45 min
✔️ THIS IS THE ALTERNATE
This pathway earns the same credit and meets the same objectives as the OptimaXR version. Complete this OR the VR assignment — not both.
📚 WHAT YOU'RE DOING
You'll build a Nuclear Decay & Stability Model with three stations — a nuclide/atom model (protons and neutrons), a band-of-stability chart, and a decay-type station (alpha, beta, gamma as balanced equations) — using a PowerPoint deck (one slide per station) or physical/drawn models you photograph. This is the first of three weekly builds; later you'll add half-life and energy (Week 2) and real radioisotope applications (Week 3), then record a short narrated walkthrough.
🎨 CHOOSE ONE FORMAT
Option A — Digital (PowerPoint)
Make a 4-slide deck: a title slide plus one slide per station (nuclide model, band-of-stability chart, decay-type station). Draw or diagram each on its slide and label it.
Option B — Hands-on (physical/drawn)
Build the nucleus from safe small objects (beads, coins, clay balls in two colors for protons/neutrons), hand-draw the band chart and the three decay equations on paper, and photograph each station. Combine the photos into one document or deck.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER · AN INVESTIGATION ARC
1
Observe & Question. Start with the phenomenon from your lessons: some nuclei stay unchanged for billions of years, while others fall apart on their own — and the band of stability sorts the two. Let this be your driving question for the build: which nuclei are stable, and when one is not, what does it emit and what does it become?
2
Predict — commit before you build. Before you make any station, choose the nuclide you will model and write a prediction on your title slide (or top of your page): is it stable or radioactive? If radioactive, which decay type do you expect, and where does it sit relative to the band (above, below, inside, or beyond Z = 83)? Write it down first — you'll test your build against it. A wrong prediction costs nothing; committing to one is the point.
3
Investigate · Station 1 — Nuclide model. Show the nucleus of the nuclide you chose with the correct count of protons and neutrons in two colors (for example carbon-12, 12 6C: 6 protons + 6 neutrons). Label which are protons and which are neutrons.
4
Investigate · Station 2 — Band-of-stability chart. Draw axes — protons (Z) across, neutrons (N) up — and sketch the diagonal band of stability, showing it bend upward (more neutrons) for heavier elements. Mark the Z = 83 ceiling beyond which nothing is stable, label where above-the-band vs below-the-band nuclei tend to decay, and plot where your chosen nuclide falls.
5
Investigate · Station 3 — Decay-type station. Write three balanced decay equations, each with its A,Z change labeled: an alpha decay (e.g. 238 92U → 234 90Th + 42He; A−4, Z−2), a beta-minus decay (e.g. 14 6C → 14 7N +  0−1e; Z+1), and a gamma emission (no change in A or Z).
6
Analyze. Check your work: for each decay equation, confirm the mass numbers (A) and atomic numbers (Z) balance on both sides. Then return to your written prediction — did your nuclide model and band chart confirm your prediction, or correct it? Note either outcome; a corrected prediction is real learning, not a mistake.
7
Defend. Add a caption to each station that makes a claim and backs it with evidence from your model: name what the station shows and give one true statement about it, backed by what's on the station (e.g., “alpha decay lowers A by 4 and Z by 2 — see the balanced equation”).
8
Extend. Save your finished model (deck or photo document). Keep this file — you'll add the half-life/energy (Week 2) and applications (Week 3) stations to it, then record a short narrated walkthrough at the end of the module.

✍️ CER REFLECTION · SUBMIT WITH YOUR MODEL
Before you submit, write a short Claim–Evidence–Reasoning paragraph about the nuclide you modeled:
▶  Claim — is your nuclide stable or radioactive (and if radioactive, which decay type)?
▶  Evidence — cite what your model shows: its N/Z or position relative to the band of stability, the Z = 83 ceiling, and/or a balanced decay equation from your station.
▶  Reasoning — explain why that evidence supports your claim, and whether it matched the prediction you committed to at the start.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
1. Upload your model file (PowerPoint .pptx, or a document/PDF of your photos) to this Canvas assignment.
2. In the comment box, write a one-sentence caption: name the nuclide you modeled and state one thing your band-of-stability chart shows.
3. Add your short CER reflection paragraph (from above) in the comment box or on a final slide/page.
