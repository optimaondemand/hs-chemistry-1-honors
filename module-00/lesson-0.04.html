📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A great course is wasted if you can't find your way around it. So before the chemistry begins: where is everything, how do you turn work in, and where do your files live?
This is the last stop on your on-ramp. You've met your VR campus, your lab-safety habits, and how scientists reason. Now you'll learn the two everyday tools that carry the course: Canvas, where every lesson, quiz, and assignment lives, and Microsoft 365, where you'll write documents and build spreadsheets. None of this is hard — it's just worth knowing on day one so you never lose a point to a misplaced file or a missed submit button. Read it once, and you'll move through the whole course smoothly.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to navigate this self-paced Canvas course — find lessons, quizzes, and assignments — and submit my work correctly.
2
I will be able to explain the course grading model, including which two items in each module are reviewed by a teacher.
3
I will be able to create, name, save, and submit a file using the course's M365 / OneDrive workflow and file-naming convention.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
Access to your Canvas account and your school Microsoft 365 sign-in (Word, Excel, and OneDrive).
▶
About 25–30 minutes. Have the course open in another tab so you can click along as you read.
📚 PART 1 · FINDING YOUR WAY AROUND CANVAS

Everything in this course is organized into Modules — the same big units you saw in the course map (Module 0 here, then Module 1, Module 2, and so on). Open the Modules page from the course menu and you'll see each module laid out top to bottom in the order you should work through it. The course is self-paced: you move through the items in sequence, at your own speed, checking each off as you go.

Inside a module you'll meet three kinds of items, each with its own little icon:

📄
Pages
Your lessons — the reading, visuals, and interactive activities, like the one you're in right now.
✅
Quizzes
Reading Checks, checkpoints, and exams. Most are auto-graded the moment you submit.
📎
Assignments
Hands-on work, VR activities, and writing — the things you turn in, usually by uploading a file or recording.

How to submit your work

Submitting is the same simple move almost every time. Open the assignment, read what it asks for, then use the Submit button. Depending on the assignment you'll either upload a file (your Word document, your VR video) or type directly into Canvas. After you submit, Canvas confirms it received your work — always wait for that confirmation so you know it went through. If something looks unsubmitted, you simply haven't finished the upload yet.

💡 A HABIT WORTH KEEPING
Work through each module in order, top to bottom. The lessons build on one another, and the Reading Check after each lesson is designed to be taken right when the reading is fresh. Self-paced doesn't mean skip-around — it means you set the speed, while the sequence stays the same.
📚 PART 2 · HOW GRADING WORKS IN THIS COURSE

It helps to know up front how your grade comes together, because most of it is in your hands and gives you instant feedback. Most of what you do is auto-graded or self-check — you find out how you did right away. A small, high-value amount is read carefully by a teacher.

AUTO-GRADED
Reading Checks & exams
Scored the instant you submit. Reading Checks are low-stakes “easy points” for keeping up.
AUTO / SELF-CHECK / COMPLETION
Weekly hands-on & VR
Your quantitative work and your smaller VR activities. Many give you a model answer to check yourself against.
TEACHER-REVIEWED — EXACTLY TWO PER MODULE
Writing assignment + VR project video
Once per module a teacher reads your one writing piece and watches your cumulative VR project video, grading each against a rubric.

So the rule to remember is simple: in each content module, exactly two items are graded by a person — the module writing assignment and the cumulative VR project video. Everything else gives you feedback automatically. (Module 0, this on-ramp, has none of the teacher-graded items — it's all auto-graded or completion-based, so you can settle in without pressure.)

⚡ TRY IT · WHO GRADES THIS?
For each item below, decide whether it is auto / self-check or whether it's one of the two teacher-reviewed items in a module. Instant feedback on each.
1. A short Reading Check after a lesson
2. The module's CER / lab-report writing assignment
3. Your end-of-module cumulative VR project video
4. A weekly hands-on calculation assignment
📚 PART 3 · YOUR FILES: THE M365 / ONEDRIVE WORKFLOW

When an assignment asks you to produce a document, you'll use Microsoft 365 — the same suite of tools you use across your school. Two of them carry almost all the work in this course:

📝 Word (.docx)
For writing — your CER responses, lab reports, and scientific arguments. The module writing assignment lives here.
📊 Excel (.xlsx)
For data — recording measurements, building tables, and graphing relationships in the data-heavy weeks.

Name every file the same way

A shared course needs tidy file names so nothing gets lost. Every file you create for this course uses the same pattern:

FILE-NAMING CONVENTION
LastName_FirstName_AssignmentName_CHEM1H
Example: Garcia_Maria_CERpractice_CHEM1H.docx

Save your course files in your OneDrive, in a folder organized as Optima → Science, so all your chemistry work lives in one place. When it's time to turn something in, you still submit through Canvas — open the assignment and use the OneDrive tab or file upload to attach your document. Saving to OneDrive keeps your file safe; submitting in Canvas is what actually hands it in. You need to do both.

📎 KEEP THIS HANDY
A full walkthrough — signing in to M365, creating your Optima → Science OneDrive folder, naming files, and attaching them in Canvas — lives in the pinned “M365 Setup & File Organization Guide.” Set this up once now and you won't have to think about it again all year.

Tap each card to check your understanding of the file workflow:

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
You finished a CER in Word. What do you name the file?
LastName_FirstName_AssignmentName_CHEM1H — e.g., Garcia_Maria_CERpractice_CHEM1H.docx. Same pattern for every course file.
⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
Where should you save your course documents?
In your OneDrive, in the Optima → Science folder — so all your chemistry work stays together and backed up.
⚡ TAP TO REVEAL
Saving to OneDrive — is that the same as turning it in?
No. Saving keeps your file safe; you still have to submit it through Canvas (OneDrive tab or file upload) and wait for the confirmation. Do both.

Work each module top to bottom in Canvas: Pages are lessons, Quizzes are checks and exams, Assignments are what you turn in.
