🔬 BEFORE THE TOOL, THE IDEA · WHY SCIENTISTS BUILD INSTRUMENTS

Before you learn how to use OptimaXR, it's worth asking why a scientist would want a tool like it at all. For most of human history, people could only investigate what their own senses could reach — and matter, the subject of this course, is mostly too small, too far, or too faint to see. So scientists built instruments to extend their senses. When Galileo turned a telescope to the night sky, he saw moons circling Jupiter that no unaided eye had ever caught. The microscope opened a living world hidden in a drop of pond water. Later, the spectroscope let scientists read the light coming off glowing substances and stars — and, astonishingly, figure out what those distant things are made of.

Each of these instruments extended human observation — but not one of them did the thinking. A telescope shows moons; it does not explain why they orbit. An instrument gathers evidence; a scientist still has to interpret it, judge how far to trust it, and reason toward an explanation. OptimaXR is the newest instrument in that long line. It lets you hold a molecule too small to ever really touch, walk around a model of an atom, and make the invisible large enough to examine. Like every instrument before it, it extends what you can observe — and, like every instrument before it, it still leaves the reasoning to you.

📚 PART 1 · WHAT OPTIMAXR IS — AND THE TWO WAYS IN

OptimaXR is Optima's spatial-computing (virtual reality) platform — the school's virtual campus. In this course it is where you'll do hands-on labs and build the projects you explain on camera. Instead of only reading about a molecule or a reaction, you'll stand next to a three-dimensional model of it, walk around it, label it, and talk through it the way a scientist would at a whiteboard. Chemistry is the study of things too small to see; OptimaXR lets you make those invisible things big enough to hold.

There are two ways into OptimaXR, and you choose the one that fits what you have:

🧐 Pathway A · VR headset
A VR headset running the OptimaXR app. You see the virtual campus all around you and use hand controllers to point, grab, and build. This is the most immersive way in.
🖥️ Pathway B · Desktop app (non-VR)
The OptimaXR desktop app on a regular computer — no headset required. You explore the same spaces with your mouse and keyboard. You can do the same work; it just appears on your screen.
💡 GOOD TO KNOW · NO ONE IS BLOCKED
Every VR assignment in this course ships with a non-VR alternate that meets the same goals. If you don't have a headset, you'll use the OptimaXR desktop app — or, where it makes sense, a short screen-recorded walkthrough you narrate yourself. The chemistry you learn and the work you turn in are the same either way. Pick the pathway that fits your situation and move forward with confidence.
📚 PART 2 · MOVING AROUND — AND STAYING SAFE

Getting around in OptimaXR is quick to learn. You look around by turning your head (in a headset) or moving your mouse (on desktop). You move through a space by teleporting or gliding to a spot you point at. And you interact with objects by pointing at them and using the grab/trigger control to pick them up, move them, or activate a tool. The exact buttons differ from one headset to another, so this lesson keeps the steps general on purpose — the precise controls for your device live in the in-headset quick-start.

💡 WHERE THE EXACT BUTTONS LIVE
For the precise button-by-button controls on your specific device, open the in-headset quick-start (the OptimaXR tutorial) the first time you enter. It walks you through pointing, grabbing, teleporting, and opening the menu hands-on — which sticks far better than reading about it. This page tells you what each control does; the quick-start shows you which button does it.

There is one setup step you should never skip in a headset: your boundary (guardian). When you first put the headset on, you'll draw or confirm the edges of your real, physical play space — the area clear of furniture, walls, and people. While you're immersed you can't see your real room, so this boundary is what warns you before you walk into a chair or a doorframe. Set it honestly, give yourself room, and clear the floor first. Safety in OptimaXR starts with your real-world space.

📚 PART 3 · THE TABLET/MENU AND YOUR TOOLKIT

Your home base inside OptimaXR is the tablet/menu — an in-world panel you open to reach your tools, join sessions, and change settings. Think of it as your control center: almost everything you'll do starts by opening the tablet/menu and choosing something from it. Get comfortable opening and closing it, because the recording tool you'll use all year lives there too.

From the tablet/menu (and your controllers) you reach the handful of core tools you'll reuse in every module. Learn these five and you can build almost anything this course asks for:

🧩 IFX object library
Spawn ready-made 3D objects into your space — atoms, spheres, lab items, and more. This is how you bring your building blocks onto the table.
✏️ 3D pen
Draw and annotate directly in the air — sketch a bond, circle a part of your model, or trace a path while you explain.
📝 Sticky notes
Drop text labels onto your work — name a molecule, mark a charge, or write the key idea so it's visible on camera.
🔒 Locking
Lock an object in place so you don't accidentally bump or move it while you build the rest of your model or record.
🎥 Recording tool
Capture a video of yourself explaining your work — the deliverable for every module project. (Part 5 walks you through it.)

Again, the exact icon or button for each tool depends on your device — let the in-headset quick-start show you where they sit, then practice each one in the VR Orientation activity at the end of this module.

📚 PART 4 · SESSIONS & LOCATIONS: WHERE YOUR WORK LIVES

OptimaXR is organized into Locations (the different virtual places — classrooms, labs, build spaces) and Sessions (a specific shared instance you join). The important thing for chemistry is that the course uses persistent Sessions: the objects you place stay put between visits. Build a model today, leave, come back tomorrow, and your work is still there waiting.

That persistence is exactly how the cumulative module projects work. In most modules you'll add to the same build week by week — a little more each time — until it's complete and you record your final explanation. Because the Session remembers your work, your project grows across the whole module instead of starting over each week. To get into the right space, you'll join from the pinned OptimaXR Sessions list (each activity tells you which Session to choose).

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · WHY PERSISTENCE MATTERS
If I leave a persistent Session in the middle of building, what happens to my work?
It stays exactly where you left it. The next time you join that same Session from the pinned Sessions list, your placed objects, notes, and drawings are still there — so you can keep adding to a project across many weeks. That is what makes a multi-week cumulative build possible.
📚 PART 5 · THE HEADLINE SKILL: CAPTURING AN IN-VR VIDEO RECORDING

Here is the one skill from this lesson you'll use most. In every module, the cumulative VR project is turned in as a video recording of you explaining your build — narrating the chemistry, walking through your model, showing a process in action. The model matters, but the graded work is your spoken explanation. So it's worth learning the recording workflow now, while the stakes are zero.

And that recording is more than a tour of your build — it's how you make a scientific explanation. A strong walkthrough does what a scientist does when presenting work: you point out what you observe in your model, you show the evidence built into it, you give the reasoning that ties your evidence to your claim, and you're honest about any uncertainty — what your model simplifies or leaves out. Keep that shape in mind every time you record, and your explanations will carry real scientific weight, not just describe what a viewer can already see.

💡 WHAT A STRONG RECORDED EXPLANATION SOUNDS LIKE
Not “here is my model.” Instead: “Notice this part — that's my observation. Here's the evidence I built in to show it. This is why that evidence supports my point — my reasoning. And here's what my model doesn't capture yet — the uncertainty.” You'll practice exactly this arc in the VR Orientation, long before it counts.
🎥 THE RECORDING WORKFLOW (GENERAL STEPS)
1
Open the tablet/menu and choose the recording tool.
2
Start the recording, then narrate while you move — point to parts of your model and explain them out loud, as if teaching someone.
3
Stop and save when you're done.
4
Find your saved file and upload it to Canvas as your assignment submission.
🔬 IF IN-HEADSET RECORDING ISN'T AVAILABLE
If your device can't capture audio inside the headset, use the screen-recording fallback: run a screen recorder on the computer or device showing your OptimaXR view (or your desktop-app view) and narrate over it. The result is the same — a video of you explaining your build — which is exactly what's graded. Check the precise control names for your device in the in-headset quick-start.

Don't aim for a perfect take. A clear, honest, one-take explanation that shows you understand the chemistry beats a polished video that says nothing. You'll rehearse this exact workflow in the VR Orientation activity — so the first time it counts, it won't be the first time you've done it.

⚡ SELF-CHECK · MATCH EACH TOOL TO WHAT IT DOES
For each tool, pick the job it's used for. Instant feedback on each — this is a no-stakes warm-up for the real thing.

💪 THE BIG IDEA TO CARRY FORWARD

OptimaXR is your newest scientific instrument — a hands-on chemistry space reachable two ways (a VR headset or the desktop app), with a non-VR alternate for every task so no one is left out. Open the tablet/menu to reach your toolkit (the IFX library to spawn objects, the 3D pen and sticky notes to annotate and label, locking to keep things put), and remember that persistent Sessions let your projects grow week by week. Above all, master the one skill that returns every module: open the recording tool and give a real scientific explanation — observe, show your evidence, reason from it, and name what's still uncertain — then save and upload. Like every instrument before it, OptimaXR extends what you can observe; the reasoning is still yours. Learn it now while it's just practice, and you'll be ready for everything ahead.
